      Your pay

      How is take-home pay worked out?

      Three things come off your pay. Income Tax: nothing on the first £12,570 a year, 20% on the next £37,700, 40% above £50,270 and 45% above £125,140. National Insurance: 8% of pay between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above. And anything you have agreed to, such as a pension or a student loan. Scotland has its own six Income Tax bands, which the Scotland button applies.

      Why is my first payslip in a new job different?

      Your tax-free allowance builds up from April whether you are working or not. If you start part way through the year, the unused allowance is waiting for you, so your first payslips have little or no Income Tax on them. National Insurance and student loan repayments are worked out on each payslip separately, so they start straight away. Add New starter to see both figures.

      What is an emergency tax code?

      If your employer does not know your tax history, HMRC can put you on a temporary code such as 1257L W1 or M1, or 0T. You may pay more tax than you owe for a month or two. Handing over a P45 or filling in the starter checklist usually fixes it, and the overpaid tax comes back through your pay.

      Do I have to join the workplace pension?

      If you are 22 or over and earn more than £10,000 a year your employer must enrol you, with at least 5% from you and 3% from them. You can opt out, but you give up the employer's money. Under 22 you can ask to join.

      Student loans

      How much student loan will I repay?

      You repay 9% of what you earn above your plan's threshold. A postgraduate loan adds 6% above its own threshold. What you owe does not change the repayment. Only your pay does.

      Which student loan plan am I on?

      It depends on where you lived and when you started. England from August 2023: Plan 5. England from 2012 to 2023, or Wales from 2012: Plan 2. Before September 2012, or Northern Ireland: Plan 1. Scotland: Plan 4. A Master's or Doctoral loan is the postgraduate plan and is repaid alongside the others.

      When do student loan repayments start?

      The April after you finish or leave your course, and only once you earn over the threshold. Your employer takes it from each payslip, so a month with a bonus can trigger a repayment even if your yearly pay is under the threshold. Anything left is written off after 25 to 40 years, depending on the plan.

      Apprenticeships and first jobs

      How much does an apprentice get paid?

      At least £8.00 an hour if you are under 19, or in the first year of your apprenticeship. After the first year, if you are 19 or over, you move to the minimum wage for your age: £10.85 for 18 to 20 and £12.71 for 21 and over. Many employers pay more. Pick "an hour" next to your pay to enter an hourly rate.

      Do apprentices pay tax?

      Yes, in the same way as any employee. On a first year apprentice wage most or all of your pay sits inside the £12,570 tax-free allowance, so there is little or no Income Tax or National Insurance to pay.

      Is an apprenticeship better than university for money?

      An apprentice earns from day one, pays no tuition fees and has no student loan to repay. A graduate starts three or four years later, often on higher pay, and repays 9% of earnings above the threshold. Which comes out ahead depends on the two salaries. The Apprenticeship or uni comparison adds up take-home pay year by year so you can see where the lines cross.

      Side business

      How is a side business taxed when I have a job?

      As a sole trader, your profit is added to your salary and taxed on top. Your salary has already used your tax-free allowance, so the profit is taxed from the first pound. If your sales are £1,000 or less in 2026/27 there is nothing to pay or report, and that limit rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027.

      Do I pay National Insurance twice with a job and a side business?

      You pay Class 1 on your salary and Class 4 on your sole trader profit, but not both in full. Profit under £12,570 pays no Class 4. Above that, an annual maximum limits the 6% rate once your job has paid enough, and the rest is charged at 2%.

      Can I claim for working from home?

      A sole trader can claim a share of rent or mortgage interest, council tax, energy, water and insurance, by room and by time, or a flat rate of £10 to £26 a month. Check your tenancy allows business use, and keep some private use of the room if you own. An employee can no longer claim from HMRC since April 2026, but an employer can pay £6 a week tax free. A limited company pays its director the same £6, or rent under a written agreement.

      When do I pay the tax on my side income?

      For 2026/27 the return and payment are due by 31 January 2028. If the bill is over £1,000 and most of your tax is not collected through your pay, HMRC also asks for payments on account in January and July. Under £3,000, you can ask for it to be collected through your tax code if you file online by 30 December.

      Is a limited company better than a sole trader?

      Less often than it used to be. A company pays Corporation Tax at 19% on profits up to £50,000, then you pay dividend tax of 10.75% or 35.75% on what you take out. A company tends to win when you leave profit in it, pay it into a pension, or need limited liability. The Sole trader or company comparison runs both on your figures.

      Limited company: the moves that matter
      • Leave profit in the company and take it in a year when your other income is lower.
      • A salary of up to £5,000 from your company carries no National Insurance and comes off profit before Corporation Tax.
      • A company pension contribution skips Corporation Tax, Income Tax and National Insurance. It counts towards your £60,000 annual allowance.
      • The first £500 of dividends each year is tax free.
      • The company can pay you £6 a week for home working with no receipts.
      • One mobile phone on a contract in the company's name is a tax free benefit.
      • Reclaim anything you buy for the business with your own money, and keep the receipts.
      • Register for VAT once sales pass £90,000 in any 12 months.
      • Budget for accounts, a Corporation Tax return and a confirmation statement every year. Corporation Tax is due nine months and one day after the year end.
      • Check your employment contract allows outside work before you start.
      What is IR35?

      IR35 is the nickname for the off-payroll working rules. If you work for a client through your own limited company but in practice work like one of their employees, the fee is taxed like a salary: Income Tax and National Insurance come off before your company is paid, and business costs no longer reduce the bill. It does not apply to sole traders.

      IR35: inside or outside?

      Pointers to outside: you decide how and when the work is done, you could send someone else, you are paid for a result and fix mistakes at your own cost, and you have other clients. Pointers to inside: the client directs the work, it has to be you, you are paid for your time and you sit in their team. A public sector or larger client decides your status and must give reasons. With a small client the decision is yours. HMRC's free Check Employment Status for Tax tool gives a view.

      Pensions

      How does pension tax relief work?

      Money going into a pension is not taxed. With a workplace pension that usually happens through your payslip. With a personal pension you pay in £80, the provider adds £20, and if you pay 40% tax you can claim another £20 back from HMRC. Salary sacrifice also saves National Insurance.

      About the numbers

      What this calculator assumes
      • One job on a standard tax code, paid evenly through the year, and no other income.
      • No child benefit charge, marriage allowance or benefits such as a company car.
      • Student loan repayments on your pay and business profit together.
      • New starter figures assume your employer has your correct tax code.
      • For a company: one director, no other staff, and sales shown without VAT.
      • For 2027/28, Scottish tax bands and the Plan 4 threshold are held at 2026/27 levels until they are announced.
